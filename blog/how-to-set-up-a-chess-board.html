How to set up a chess board correctly

Two rules cover it: light square on the right, and the queen goes on her own colour. Almost every setup mistake is one of these two.

RulesBeginner

More beginner games start from a wrong position than anyone admits, and it matters more than it sounds. Castling, en passant and most opening theory assume the board is the right way round.

There are only two rules to remember.

Rule one: light square on the right

Each player should have a light-coloured square in the nearest right-hand corner. For White that is h1, for Black it is a8.

If the near-right corner is dark, turn the whole board ninety degrees. Do not move the pieces, turn the board.

The old rhyme is "light on right". It is worth saying out loud with a child the first dozen times, because it becomes automatic quickly.

Rule two: the queen goes on her own colour

The white queen starts on a light square, d1. The black queen starts on a dark square, d8. The kings take the square beside them, e1 and e8.

This is the rule people get wrong, usually by swapping the king and queen. The check is instant: if your queen is not standing on her own colour, you have the king and queen the wrong way round.

The two queens end up facing each other down the d-file, which is a useful visual confirmation that both sides are set up correctly.

Everything else

Working from the corners inwards on the back rank: rook, knight, bishop, queen, king, bishop, knight, rook. Pawns fill the whole second rank in front.

The pieces mirror each other. Whatever White has on a1, Black has on a8.

The mistakes we see most

The board turned the wrong way. Kings and queens end up on the wrong files, and everything a child has learned about castling looks wrong.

King and queen swapped. Especially common when a child sets up White correctly by copying a diagram, then sets up Black as a mirror image of White rather than a rotation.

Knights and bishops swapped. Less damaging but it makes early development feel strange, because the knight is supposed to be the piece that can jump out first.

A quick check before every game

Three seconds, every time:

  • Light square in my near-right corner?
  • Is my queen on her own colour?
  • Are the two queens facing each other?

If all three are true, the board is right. Teach a child to run that check themselves and they will never start from a wrong position again.
