After enough lessons you stop seeing individual mistakes and start seeing the same ten, in roughly the same order, in almost every new player. Here they are, with what to do instead.

## 1. Bringing the queen out on move two

The queen is the strongest piece, so beginners reach for it immediately. Every developing move the opponent makes also attacks it, and the queen spends the opening running away while the opponent builds a position.

**Instead:** knights and bishops first. The queen comes out when the board has opened for her.

## 2. Moving the same piece over and over

Three moves with one knight is three moves not spent developing three pieces.

**Instead:** in the opening, move each piece once before moving any piece twice, unless there is a concrete reason.

## 3. Not castling

Beginners leave the king in the centre because castling feels like a wasted move. Then a file opens, and the game is over in four moves.

**Instead:** castle within the first ten moves unless something urgent stops you.

## 4. Grabbing every free pawn

A pawn that looks free at the edge of the board often costs three tempi to collect, and the opponent uses them.

**Instead:** ask what you give up to take it.

## 5. Ignoring what the opponent just did

This is the single biggest one. Beginners plan their own attack and never ask what the other player is threatening.

**Instead:** after every opponent move, one question. *What does that move attack?*

## 6. Attacking with one piece

A lone queen invading the opponent's position gets chased away. Attacks need two or three attackers.

**Instead:** bring another piece before committing.

## 7. Trading pieces for no reason

Trades are not neutral. Every exchange helps one side. Trading when you are behind on material makes the deficit worse.

**Instead:** trade when you are ahead in material, or when it relieves pressure.

## 8. Pushing pawns in front of the king

Those pawns are the king's shelter. Once they move, they cannot come back.

**Instead:** leave them unless there is a reason, with the small exception of giving the king an escape square against back-rank mates.

## 9. Playing fast

Most lost pieces are lost to moves played in under two seconds.

**Instead:** before touching a piece, check whether the square is defended.

## 10. Never reviewing a loss

The habit that separates children who improve from children who stall. A game you lose and never look at teaches you nothing.

**Instead:** one question after every loss. *Which move was the mistake?*
