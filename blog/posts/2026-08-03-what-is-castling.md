Castling is the one move in chess where two pieces move at the same time. The king travels two squares towards a rook, and that rook jumps over to the square the king passed. It exists for one reason: to get the king out of the centre before the position opens.

## The four conditions

Castling is legal only if **all four** are true.

1. **Neither the king nor that rook has moved.** Moving a rook and putting it back does not reset this.
2. **The squares between them are empty.**
3. **The king is not currently in check.**
4. **The king does not pass through, or land on, a square that is attacked.**

Note what is *not* on the list: the rook may pass through an attacked square, and the rook may be attacked. Only the king's journey matters. This surprises almost everyone.

## Kingside and queenside

**Kingside** (short castling) moves the king two squares towards the h-file rook. It is quicker, needs only two squares cleared, and leaves the king tucked behind three untouched pawns.

**Queenside** (long castling) moves the king towards the a-file rook and needs three squares cleared. The king ends a little more exposed, but the rook lands on a central file immediately, which is often worth more.

## When to castle

**Early.** Most strong players castle within the first ten moves. Beginners leave it too late and get punished when a central file opens.

The main reason to delay is if you are certain the position will stay closed, or if you are not yet sure which side is safer.

## Which side

A rough guide:

- **Castle on the side where your pawns are intact.** A king behind advanced pawns has no shelter.
- **Castle away from the opponent's attack.** If they have pieces massed on the kingside, going queenside removes the target.
- **Opposite-side castling means a race.** If you castle queenside and they castle kingside, both sides throw pawns at each other's king, and whoever arrives first usually wins. It is sharp and not for the faint-hearted.

## The most common error

Castling into an attack. A child sees "castle early" as an absolute rule and castles kingside directly into three enemy pieces aimed at h7. The rule is a default, not a law. Look at what is pointing at the square first.

## The second most common error

Moving the king or rook "just for one move" and losing castling rights forever. There is no way to get them back.
